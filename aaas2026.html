UAV Imagery Labeling: Solutions and Challenges

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have emerged as a critical technology across different domains, including healthcare, security, and commercial applications. UAV Imagery serves as an essential data source; however, its characteristics, including resolution, altitude, and field of view, introduce challenging variability. One of the major concerns lies in object detection and accurate image labeling. Current studies focus on detecting pre-labeled datasets and pre-trained models; these approaches struggle to generalize effectively. Issues such as low resolution, small-scale targets, and cluttered or complex backgrounds degrade detection accuracy and often require costly model training. In this work, we investigate the efficiency and accuracy of current object detection and auto-labeling techniques and demonstrate their capabilities and limitations when it comes to small objects captured by high-altitude UAV images. We benchmark several models using a newly curated dataset developed in collaboration with the Public Authority for Civil Information (PACI) in Kuwait. This dataset contains 524 preprocessed and aggregated images of five distinct classes, representing a diverse urban aerial environment. Our preliminary findings reveal that the DINO-SAM auto-labeling model achieved a Mean Average Precision (mAP) of 9\%, a precision of 32.84\%, and a recall of 3.26\%, which indicates very low sensitivity and specificity. Further analysis shows significant class imbalance, where one dominant class affects model learning and impacts detection errors for underrepresented classes. These results show the limitations of current labeling and detection models when faced with new or complex UAV datasets, especially for small-object recognition. Introducing novel datasets not only highlights these shortcomings but also opens the door for model refinement, data augmentation strategies, and multi-dataset training approaches, which can reduce false negatives and improve detection reliability. Our goal is to drive progress toward robust and generalizable UAV object detection systems.
